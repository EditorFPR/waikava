The ship

H.M.S. Nymphe — composite screw sloop, Royal Navy

On 10 November 1876 H.M.S. Nymphe slipped her moorings at Levuka and stood out to sea, carrying Sir Arthur Gordon, the first Governor of Fiji, on the most delicate mission of his administration — a great council of the chiefs of the north and east, to be held at Waikava in Cakaudrove, on Vanua Levu.

She was a small ship for so large an errand: a composite screw sloop of the Royal Navy, barque-rigged, steaming when the wind failed and sailing when it did not. Her voyage out threaded the Lau Group — Matuku, Totoya, Moala, Lomaloma, Vanua Balavu — pausing at Ma'afu's own islands before turning west for Taveuni and the council ground.

The islands

10 Nov · departure

Levuka, Ovalau

The old capital clings to its strip of coast beneath the cliffs. H.M.S. Nymphe departs from here, the voyage's beginning and, a month later, its end at Nasova nearby.

South of the route

Kadavu

Lying well south of Nymphe's track — marked here for bearings, not for anchoring.

19 Nov · Vuna

Taveuni

The garden island. At Vuna, on its southern shore, the Beagle is found — a small drama in the record.

13 Nov

Totoya

Ma'afu's village, on a drowned volcanic crater ringed by reef. Charted here from Admiralty chart no. 1248.

14 Nov

Moala

Another of Ma'afu's islands — the Tui Lau's country, where the Nymphe pays her respects before the council.

The Lau passage

The sea road

Reef, palm and trade wind — the corridor the Nymphe worked east to Lomaloma and Vanua Balavu, where Ma'afu received the Governor and showed him the schools.

Vanua Levu & Taveuni

The north

Where the council waited — Waikava, Cakaudrove, twenty minutes' sail from these same green coasts.
